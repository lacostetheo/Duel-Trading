    Basics

    Reading a candle

    Each candle = 1 minute. Green: the price rose (close above open). Red: it fell. The wicks reach the highest and lowest prices traded.

    Support, resistance, range

    A support is a level where the price bounces up, a resistance a level where it stalls. In between, the price swings back and forth: a range. Buy near support, sell near resistance — until the breakout.

    Trend

    Higher highs and higher lows = uptrend. Lower highs and lower lows = downtrend. Trading in the direction of the trend is easier.

    SL, TP and leverage

    The stop-loss closes the position if the market moves against you, the take-profit locks in the gain. Aim for a reward at least 1.5 times the risk. Leverage multiplies gains AND losses: at ×10, a 1% drop costs 10% of the margin.

    Tools

    RSI

    Measures the speed of recent moves, from 0 to 100. Above 70: overbought, the rally may be running out of steam. Below 30: oversold. Not a signal on its own: in a strong trend, RSI can stay extreme for a long time.

    Moving averages and Bollinger

    The 20 MA follows the short-term trend, the 50 MA the underlying one. Price above them = buyers in control; when the 20 crosses the 50, momentum shifts. Bollinger Bands widen in volatile markets and tighten before big moves.

    Order book and liquidity

    The book shows who wants to buy (green) and sell (red) at each price. A large wall slows the price down. The bigger the order, the more levels it “eats”: that is slippage. Liquidity zones are where stops cluster, and price often goes looking for them.

    Pending orders and management

    Limit: buy cheaper (at support) or sell higher. Stop: enter on a breakout. Once in profit: stop to entry (no more loss possible), ½ to bank half, trailing stop to let the winner run.
